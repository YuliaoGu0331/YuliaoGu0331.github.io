# [Ph.D. in PKU]-1

{% include published-date.html date=page.date %}

<div class="bilingual-passage">
  <p lang="zh-CN">两个月的暑假瞬间就过完了，尽管从广义上说大四的下半学期我几乎都处于一个放假的状态，但真的处于“假期”中与处于“假期”状态是两码事。从7月2号毕业拿到双证回家到现在报道前夜，并没有那么遥远。</p>
  <p lang="en">The two-month summer break passed in an instant. In a broad sense, I had been more or less on vacation throughout the second half of my senior year, but actually being on holiday and merely living as though one were on holiday are two very different things. The day I graduated, received both of my certificates, and returned home on July 2 does not feel so far away from this eve of registration.</p>
</div>

<div class="bilingual-passage">
  <p lang="zh-CN">换做高中/大二大三之前的我，对于我昏昏碌碌的暑假，想必会非常自责与后悔吧。我是一个内耗很严重的人，我的自我机制总会习惯性地将他人纳入自己比较的范围。现在这种情况已经好了很多，但不免有时候突然发作。好在暑假多少看了几本书，学了点课程，至于学进去多少，学了有没有用，这种事我懒得去想。</p>
  <p lang="en">Had this been the version of me from high school—or from before my sophomore and junior years of college—I would probably have felt deeply guilty and regretful about such a hazy, aimless summer. I am prone to intense inner conflict, and my mind habitually draws other people into its frame of comparison. Things have improved considerably, though the old tendency still flares up from time to time. At least I read a few books and took a few courses over the summer. How much I actually learned, and whether any of it will prove useful, are questions I cannot be bothered to dwell on.</p>
</div>

<div class="bilingual-passage">
  <p lang="zh-CN">从杭州东出发四五个小时的列车到北京东，没有夏令营来的时候那么疲惫。我爸的朋友随即接待了我们，请我们吃了北京烤鸭（还是挺对我胃口的）。</p>
  <p lang="en">The four- or five-hour train journey from Hangzhou East to Beijing East did not leave me as exhausted as the trip here for the summer camp. A friend of my father met us soon afterward and treated us to Peking duck—which suited my taste rather well.</p>
</div>

<div class="bilingual-passage">
  <p lang="zh-CN">宾馆的网络很差，但能够勉强接受。明天就要报道了。我没有想象中的那么兴奋与紧张，可能是因为没有第一次考上复旦时候那种命运感与跨越感吧。</p>
  <p lang="en">The hotel Wi-Fi is poor, though just about usable. Tomorrow is registration day. I am not as excited or nervous as I had imagined I would be—perhaps because this moment lacks the same sense of destiny and of crossing a threshold that I felt when I was first admitted to Fudan.</p>
</div>
